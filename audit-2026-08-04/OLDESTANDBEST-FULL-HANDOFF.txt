1. THE PROJECT
================================================================================

oldestandbest.com is an evidence database for 51 contested New Testament
readings (textual variants). For each passage it lays out Greek manuscripts,
Latin witnesses, ancient versions, church-father citations, and printed
editions, with dates, and takes the position that the KJV/TR reading should be
retained.

Tech: Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, deployed via OpenNext/Cloudflare.
Data lives in `src/data/`, principally:
  - passages.ts               (Wave 1: 30 passages, hand-authored)
  - wave2.generated.json      (Wave 2: 21 passages, generated, better quality)
  - wave2Passages.ts          (merges Wave 2 into the Passage[] type)
  - derived.ts                (allPassages = passages.map(corrections).sort())
  - types.ts                  (Passage, Witness, PatristicWitness)
  - kjvForwardCorrections.ts  (existing corrections pipeline — follow this pattern)
  - latinRepresentativeWitnesses*.ts (named Old Latin witnesses for 34 passages)

THE OWNER'S REQUIREMENT (verbatim intent)
  Every passage should be fully accounted for in all witnesses — Greek, Latin,
  versional, patristic — with accurate dates and a source for every claim, so a
  reader can open e.g. 1 John 5:7 and see the whole evidentiary record and how
  old each piece is. No false or invented data. Where a number is given it must
  be real and sourced.

2.1 EXTRACTING THE SITE'S OWN DATA (ground truth, not scraped pages)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The passage pages render witness tables client-side, so fetching HTML returns
only tab labels. Instead, compile the real data model:

  Write a Node script that registers a TypeScript require-hook using the repo's
  own `typescript` package (node_modules/typescript), maps the "@/..." alias to
  <root>/src, then:

      const { allPassages } = require('<root>/src/data/derived.ts');
      process.stdout.write(JSON.stringify(allPassages));

  This yields all 51 passages exactly as rendered, Wave 1 + Wave 2 merged, with
  applyKjvForwardCorrections applied. Total: 2,078 evidence rows
  (1,957 witness rows + 121 patristic rows).

2.2 HARVESTING A FULL APPARATUS — LaParola (THE KEY RESOURCE)
--------------------------------------------------------------
laparola.net/greco/ hosts a per-verse critical apparatus for the WHOLE New
Testament: readings, witnesses (Greek/versional/patristic), each with a DATE and
a TEXT-TYPE. Free. This is the single most useful source found.

  IMPORTANT: the form is POST-ONLY. GET returns an empty page.

  Endpoint:  https://www.laparola.net/greco/index.php   (POST, form-encoded)
  Fields:
      rif1       book code (see below)
      rif2       "chapter:verse"      e.g. "9:29"
      VisTesto   "View text"
      varianti   "s"    (YES — show variants; "1" silently returns nothing)
      allusioni  "s"    (YES — show patristic allusions)
      ord        "2"    (order witnesses BY DATE)
      msstt      "n"    (print manuscript info after the siglum, not as tooltip)
      wh,tisch,biz,inter  "n"
      direzione  "v"
      lin        "en"

  Book codes for rif1:
      47 Matthew   48 Mark      49 Luke      50 John       51 Acts
      52 Romans    53 1Cor      54 2Cor      55 Galatians  56 Ephesians
      57 Phil      58 Colossians 59 1Thess   60 2Thess     61 1Timothy
      62 2Timothy  63 Titus     64 Philemon  65 Hebrews    66 James
      67 1Peter    68 2Peter    69 1John     70 2John      71 3John
      72 Jude      73 Revelation

  Output format (one line per reading):
      <reading>] <siglum> {Date <d>; Text type <t>} <siglum> {…} …

  Parse with:
      /([^\s{}]+)\s*\{([^}]*)\}/g     and    /Date\s+([^;]+)/

  Notes:
   - Filter out modern editions/translations: WH, Riv, CEI, Nv, TILC, NM, NR,
     ND, Dio, NA, ς, Byz2005, lAD, Erasmus.
   - Normalise sigla: strip leading "(" / "[", trailing *, c, vid, mg, text,
     supp, fractions (1/2), and (pt)/(ms)/(mss).
   - LaParola lists ALL variant units in a verse, so a raw witness count for a
     verse is larger than the count relevant to one disputed unit.

  5. Matthew 6:13: "approximately 98%+" — no collation of Matthew at this point
     has been published. Label as estimate.
  6. Matthew 17:21: "about 99.4 percent of ~1,700 manuscripts of Matthew." The
     10 omitting witnesses are solid. ~1,700 is defensible but means
     "manuscripts attesting a given Gospel verse," not "manuscripts of Matthew,"
     and is a round estimate — so 99.4% is false precision. Use "more than 99%."

  A NOTE ON COUNTING STANDARDS
  The owner originally asked for figures shaped like "approx. 5,500 of 9,000
  Latin witnesses from AD 100–900." No such figure can be produced honestly for
  any verse. Only ~80–90 Old Latin manuscripts survive for the whole NT; the
  ~10,000 Vulgate manuscripts are overwhelmingly 9th century and later and have
  never been collated verse-by-verse; nothing Latin survives from AD 100.
  Exact Greek "X of Y" counts exist only where Text und Textwert or ECM has
  collated every manuscript at that point — and there is NO ECM for Matthew,
  Luke, Romans, Colossians or 1 Timothy. The defensible alternative is to name
  witnesses individually (which LaParola makes possible for all 51) and to state
  plainly what has and has not been collated.

## 4. The pattern

Three kinds of evidence are systematically absent across nearly every passage:

**The Diatessaron (2nd century).** It appears in LaParola for at least 20 of your 51 passages and is almost never on your site. **Church fathers.** Your 121 patristic rows across 51 passages, against LaParola's several hundred. Eighteen of your passages have no fathers at all. For a site whose organising question is *how old is this reading*, this is the biggest single gap.

**Named minuscules and lectionaries.** Your pages stop at the famous uncials and a handful of minuscules, then say "Byzantine Majority" or "1,500+ manuscripts." LaParola names 40–80 individual witnesses with dates for the same verses. **Replacing your aggregate rows with these named lists is the single change that would most improve the site** — and it directly answers what you originally asked for, because it turns an unverifiable "1,500+" into a list a reader can check.

## 5. On the counting standard you set

You asked for figures shaped like *"approx. 5,500 of 9,000 Latin witnesses from 100 AD to 900 AD."* No figure of that shape can be produced honestly, for any verse — not because the research is hard, but because the quantities don't exist. Publishing in that format would put fabrications on the site.

**Latin.** Roughly **80–90** Old Latin manuscripts survive for the whole New Testament. Most are fragmentary; a given verse is typically attested in 3–15, few enough to name individually — which your `latinRepresentativeWitnesses*.ts` files already do well for 34 passages. The Vulgate survives in perhaps **10,000** manuscripts, overwhelmingly 9th century and later, with no verse-by-verse collation of all of them ever published. Wordsworth–White and the Stuttgart Vulgate collate a selected 20–40 of the oldest; those are the citable ones. **Nothing Latin survives from AD 100** — the Latin versions begin late in the second century at the earliest and the oldest Latin manuscripts are fourth century.

**Greek.** Exact "X of Y" counts exist only where every known manuscript has been collated at that point: *Text und Textwert* (at its selected test passages only) or ECM (Catholic Letters, Acts, Mark, John in progress). **There is no ECM for Matthew, Luke, Romans, Colossians or 1 Timothy** — 17 of your 51 passages.

**Fathers.** A father counts only where a citation preserving the disputed wording survives, checked in a critical edition. Silence is not evidence. Two traps: parallel passages (Matthew 17:21 / Mark 9:29 is the live one on your site — you currently assign eleven fathers to Matthew and none to Mark), and paraphrase, which usually cannot settle word order.

**The honest alternative** is what wave 2 already does: name witnesses individually, and where you must aggregate, say what has actually been collated. *"Supported by the great majority of Greek manuscripts; no complete collation of this verse has been published"* is a **stronger** claim than an invented percentage, because it cannot be knocked down.

### 1.3 The "Site Summary Score" converts rows into votes — METHOD

On 1 John 5:7 the site prints:

> **16.7% for / 83.3% against**
> *"A site-level summary from the Greek manuscript count plus the Latin, Syriac, Coptic, patristic, lectionary, and printed evidence listed for this passage."*

This is a percentage derived from how many rows you have entered, not from the evidence. One church father is one row. The entire Byzantine tradition is one row. Five marginal Greek notes are five rows. The number moves whenever you add a row, which means it measures your data entry, not the manuscript record.

**Recommendation:** remove the percentage, or relabel it unambiguously as "share of records stored on this page" and detach it from the words "for" and "against." As written, a reader will reasonably take 16.7% to be a statement about the strength of the evidence for the Comma. It is not.

## Part 3 — On the standard you set for witness counts

You asked for counts in this form: *"approx. 5,500 of 9,000 Latin witnesses from 100 AD to 900 AD."*

I need to be direct with you: **no figure of that shape can be produced honestly, for any verse.** Not because the research is hard, but because the underlying quantities don't exist. If the site publishes numbers in that format they will be fabrications, and that is the opposite of what you're trying to build.

Here is the actual situation, by witness class.

### Latin

- **Old Latin (Vetus Latina):** roughly **80–90** manuscripts survive in total, across the whole New Testament. Not thousands. Beuron's register assigns each a number; most are fragmentary, and most individual verses are attested in only a handful. For a given passage you will typically be able to name between 3 and 15 Old Latin witnesses **individually** — which is far better than a percentage anyway.
- **Vulgate:** approximately **10,000** manuscripts survive, but the overwhelming majority are **9th century and later**. There is no published verse-by-verse collation of all of them. Wordsworth–White (Gospels/Acts) and the Stuttgart Vulgate collate a selected group of the oldest and best — on the order of **20–40** manuscripts. Those are the ones you can actually cite.
- **Nothing survives from AD 100.** The Latin versions begin in the later second century at the earliest, and the oldest surviving Latin *manuscripts* are fourth century. A range labelled "from 100 AD" would be wrong on its face.

**Recommended format instead:** name the Old Latin witnesses individually by siglum with dates, then state the Vulgate position as a tradition with the collated evidence behind it. That is both more accurate and more useful to a reader than any percentage.

### Greek

Exact "X of Y" counts exist only where someone has collated every known manuscript at that exact point. Two projects do this:

- **Text und Textwert** (INTF, de Gruyter) — collates *every* known Greek manuscript at selected test passages. Where one of your 51 variants coincides with a *Teststelle*, you get a genuinely exact number. Where it doesn't, no exact number exists.
- **ECM** — full apparatus, but only for the books published: Catholic Letters, Acts, Mark, and John (in progress). **There is no ECM for Matthew, Luke, Romans, Colossians, or 1 Timothy**, which covers a large share of your 51.

Everywhere else, an honest page says something like "supported by the great majority of Greek manuscripts; no complete collation of this verse has been published" — and that is a *stronger* position than a made-up percentage, because it cannot be knocked down.

### Church fathers

A father can only be counted when there is an actual citation or clear allusion preserving the disputed wording, checked in a critical edition of that father. Silence is not evidence and cannot be counted. Two specific traps:

- Parallel passages. A father quoting "prayer and fasting" may be quoting **Matthew 17:21**, not Mark 9:29. This affects several of your 51 directly.
- Loose citation. Many patristic quotations are paraphrastic and cannot settle word order or a single particle.

BiblIndex is the right tool for *locating* patristic references, but it is an index, not an apparatus — it tells you where a father discusses a verse, not what his text read.
